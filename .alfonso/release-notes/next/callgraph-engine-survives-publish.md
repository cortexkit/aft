- A checkout's call-graph engine now carries over each publication of the checkout's changes. Before, the engine was dropped and the next edit's refresh built a new one from the publication. The engine keeps every file it has bound and is moved onto the new publication, but only when it resolved exactly what the publication holds; otherwise it starts again as before. On a replay of 430 real edits to AFT's repository, published as AFT publishes them (88 publications), refreshes started an engine once instead of 88 times. They used about a third less CPU (1,291 s against 868 s), took about the same wall time (+2%), and the process peaked at 3.8-4.2 GB instead of 5.4-5.5 GB. An engine is released after 10 minutes without an edit, when the engines of all checkouts together hold more than 1 GiB (the least recently used goes first), or when its checkout is unbound. Each release is logged with counts by reason. The first refresh after a bind, a restart or a release still takes 5-15% more CPU than before the change that cut its memory.
